The Great Barter Hustle

Two identical markets. Twelve traders in each. Will barter succeed?

This activity will take place in the next lesson.

Lesson 2: vocabulary · 20 minutes, then Barter Hustle · 20 minutes.

Prepare 4 Trade 6 Compare 2 Discuss 6 Exit 2

Market A · Teal cards

12 students play on one side of the room. Trade only with other teal-card traders.

Market B · Amber cards

12 students play on the other side. Trade only with other amber-card traders.

How to play

  1. Go to your market: A (teal) or B (amber). Stay in that market.
  2. Read your starting item, its condition and your final goal. Keep your card.
  3. Ask what others currently have. You may accept a temporary item and trade it again.
  4. After every agreed swap, both traders update their current item and transaction record. Keep trading until you reach your goal or time ends.
  5. Use at least two phrases and two target words. Be honest about condition. You may refuse. No money or real objects are used.
  6. At the bell, tick your result: direct deal, chain deal or no deal.

Teacher guide · prepare before class

Prepare · 4 minutes: place Market A on one side and Market B on the other. Give one teal card to each of 12 Ss in A and the matching amber set to 12 Ss in B. Model one direct trade and one temporary trade.

Trade · 6 minutes: start both markets together. Ss may trade more than once, but only inside their colour group. Prompt them to update “current item” after every transaction.

Compare · 2 minutes: record direct deals, chain deals and no deals for A and B in the table.

Discuss · 6 minutes: ask for one direct-deal story, one chain story and one failed-deal story. Use the questions above to lead Ss toward the usefulness of a shared currency without telling them the conclusion first.

Exit · 2 minutes: Ss complete the two sentence frames above.

Teacher-only card logic: direct pairs 1↔6 and 4↔10; chains 3→9→12→3 and 5→7→11→5; cards 2 and 8 should fail because an empty power bank does not meet the goal of a fully charged one and an incomplete puzzle does not meet the goal of a complete one.

Smaller classes: preserve complete structures—a whole direct pair, a whole three-card chain or the whole failed pair. Never remove one role from a chain. If numbers are uneven, two Ss can share one role.
